NCA Policy Changes 2026: Indigenous Law Requirement & Language Screening Explained

By Kartik Kumar · 9 min read · Updated: March 2026

NCA policy changes effective March 1, 2026 — the new mandatory Indigenous Law competency, language screening before assessment, and the 2029 in-person education change.

The short answer: Three changes to the NCA assessment process took effect on March 1, 2026. The most important one for current candidates is the new mandatory Indigenous Law and Peoples competency — it applies to virtually all applicants and must be completed before your Certificate of Qualification will be issued. This guide covers each change, who it affects, and exactly what you need to do.

Three significant changes to the NCA assessment process took effect on March 1, 2026. If you applied for an NCA assessment recently, are about to apply, or are currently working through your requirements, at least one of these changes affects you directly.

Quick Answer: Does This Apply to You?

Your Situation What Changed
Assessment issued on or after March 1, 2026 Indigenous Law competency is mandatory for your COQ
Applying now for the first time All three changes apply — read in full below
From a non-English/French legal jurisdiction Language screening must now be completed before assessment
Planning law school abroad The 2029 in-person rule affects program choices you make today

Change 1: Indigenous Law and Peoples — Now Mandatory

What changed

All NCA applicants must now demonstrate competency in Indigenous Law and Peoples as part of the qualification process. This is a standalone mandatory requirement — it is not an NCA exam subject, and it does not change the number of exams on your assessment letter.

The competency covers:

  • History and legacy of residential schools in Canada
  • The UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP)
  • Aboriginal and treaty rights under section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982
  • Truth and Reconciliation Commission Calls to Action
  • Crown-Indigenous relations and the duty to consult
  • Systemic discrimination in the Canadian legal system

How to satisfy it

Route A — CPLED Online Module

The Federation of Law Societies has partnered with CPLED to offer an online Indigenous Law and Peoples module. It is self-paced, approximately 8–12 hours of study, and can be completed from anywhere. Visit cpled.ca for registration details and current pricing.

Route B — Approved Law School Course

If you have completed a Canadian law school course covering Indigenous law to the required standard, you may apply for exemption. Submit the course outline for NCA review. Not all Indigenous law courses at all schools will qualify — confirm with the NCA before assuming an exemption applies.

Frequently asked questions about the Indigenous Law requirement

Does this apply to my assessment if it was issued before March 1?
Most candidates assessed before March 1, 2026 will still need to satisfy the Indigenous Law and Peoples competency before receiving their Certificate of Qualification. Check the NCA's transitional provisions or contact the NCA directly to confirm whether the requirement applies to your specific assessment letter.
Does it count as one of my NCA exam subjects?
No. It is a separate mandatory requirement that sits entirely outside the NCA examination process. It does not replace or add to the exam subjects on your assessment letter.
How long does the CPLED module take?
Approximately 8–12 hours, self-paced online. You can complete it alongside your NCA exam preparation. Check the CPLED website (cpled.ca) for current pricing and registration details.
When should I complete it?
Complete it early — in parallel with your exam subjects. Do not leave it until after you have passed all your exams. It must be completed before the NCA will issue your Certificate of Qualification, so leaving it to the end adds unnecessary delay after you have already done the harder work.

Change 2: Language Screening Before Assessment

What changed

Language screening must now be completed before the NCA issues an assessment decision. Previously this could happen later in the process.

If you are applying from a jurisdiction where English or French is not the official language of legal practice, you must submit approved language test results as part of your application package. Your application will not proceed to assessment without them.

Approved tests include IELTS, CELPIP, TEF Canada, and TCF Canada, subject to NCA minimum score thresholds.

Who is affected

Candidates whose legal education and practice was primarily in a language other than English or French. Candidates from English-speaking common law jurisdictions (UK, India, Nigeria, Ghana, Australia, Singapore, etc.) where English is the official language of legal practice are typically not required to submit additional testing — but confirm with the NCA when applying.

Practical impact on your timeline

Build in 2–4 weeks for test scheduling, writing, and results delivery before your application can progress. If you are planning to apply soon and this requirement applies to you, arrange the test now.

Change 3: In-Person Education Tightening From 2029

What is changing

Effective January 1, 2029, interactive online instruction will no longer count toward the two-thirds in-person education threshold required for NCA assessment eligibility. Only physically in-person instruction will count.

This does not affect most current candidates

If you have already graduated from law school, this change does not affect your assessment. Your education is assessed under the rules in force when you studied.

Who needs to act now

Anyone currently choosing which overseas law school to attend, or considering a hybrid or online law degree with the intention of later applying for NCA assessment. If your program has a significant online component, verify the in-person percentage now. If it falls below two-thirds, your NCA eligibility could be affected after 2029.

Your Action Checklist

  • Check your assessment letter date. If issued on or after March 1, 2026, the Indigenous Law requirement applies.
  • Register for the CPLED Indigenous Law and Peoples module as soon as possible — complete it in parallel with your exam subjects.
  • Confirm language screening status. If from a non-English/French jurisdiction, arrange your language test now — it must be submitted before your application proceeds to assessment.
  • If choosing a law school abroad, verify the in-person component exceeds two-thirds of total instruction hours.
  • If you have completed all NCA exams, confirm your Indigenous Law status before applying for your COQ.

What This Means for Your NCA Exam Preparation

The core exam subjects — Administrative Law, Constitutional Law, Criminal Law, Foundations of Canadian Law, Professional Responsibility — have not changed in content or format. Your preparation strategy remains the same.

What changes is the overall completion pathway. You now have one additional mandatory step. Treat the Indigenous Law and Peoples module as part of your overall plan from day one — not something to add at the end.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the new Indigenous Law requirement apply if my assessment was issued before March 1, 2026?
Most candidates assessed before March 1, 2026 will still need to satisfy the Indigenous Law and Peoples competency before receiving their Certificate of Qualification. The NCA has transitional provisions covering pre-existing assessments. Check the NCA's official transitional guidance or contact the NCA directly at nca.legal to confirm whether the requirement applies to your specific assessment letter.
Does the Indigenous Law module count as one of my NCA exam subjects?
No. The Indigenous Law and Peoples competency is a separate mandatory requirement that sits entirely outside the NCA examination process. It does not replace any exam subject on your assessment letter, nor does it add a new exam subject. It must be completed through the CPLED online module (or an approved law school course) before your Certificate of Qualification will be issued.
How long does the CPLED Indigenous Law module take to complete?
The CPLED Indigenous Law and Peoples module is self-paced and takes approximately 8–12 hours to complete. It is available online and can be completed from anywhere, in parallel with your NCA exam preparation. Check cpled.ca for current pricing and registration details.
Who does the language screening requirement affect?
Language screening applies to candidates whose legal education and practice was primarily in a language other than English or French. Candidates from English-speaking common law jurisdictions — such as the UK, India, Nigeria, Ghana, Australia, and Singapore — where English is the official language of legal practice are typically not required to submit additional testing. Confirm your specific status directly with the NCA when applying.
Does the 2029 in-person education change affect me if I have already graduated from law school?
No. If you have already graduated from law school, the 2029 in-person education change does not affect your NCA assessment. Your education is assessed under the rules in force when you studied. The change only affects candidates currently choosing which law school to attend or those considering a hybrid or online law degree with the intention of later applying for NCA assessment.
When should I complete the Indigenous Law module — before or after my NCA exams?
Complete the CPLED Indigenous Law and Peoples module early — in parallel with your NCA exam subjects. Do not leave it until after you have passed all your exams. The module must be completed before the NCA will issue your Certificate of Qualification, so leaving it to the end will add unnecessary delay after you have already finished the harder work of passing the exams.
