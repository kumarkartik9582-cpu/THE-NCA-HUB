You have bills, perhaps a family, and certainly responsibilities. Preparing for the NCA exams must fit around your life rather than consume it entirely. This schedule is designed for **working professionals preparing for NCA exams** with limited daily time. It assumes **2 hours of focused study on weekdays and longer sessions on weekends**, allowing consistent preparation while maintaining full-time employment. --- ## Recommended NCA Study Schedule (Quick Overview) **Weekdays:** 2 hours per day **Weekends:** 4 hours Saturday + 4 hours Sunday **Total weekly study time:** ~18 hours **Typical preparation time per subject:** 10–12 weeks For many candidates, this level of consistency is sufficient preparation for one NCA subject when used efficiently. --- ## How Long Does It Take to Prepare for NCA Exams While Working? Most candidates preparing while employed full-time spend **80–120 hours preparing for each NCA exam subject**. A schedule that includes: - **2 hours per weekday** - **8 hours across the weekend** produces approximately **18 hours of preparation per week**. Over **12 weeks**, this results in roughly **216 hours of total preparation time**. If you are unsure how many hours you need, see our guide on **NCA study hours and preparation time**. --- ## The Working Professional Reality Most candidates balancing work and NCA preparation have the following realistic schedule available: **Weekdays:** 2 hours (for example, 7:00–9:00 PM or 5:00–7:00 AM) **Saturday:** 4 hours **Sunday:** 4 hours Total available preparation time is typically **around 18 hours per week**. With consistent effort over **10–12 weeks**, this can provide sufficient preparation time for one NCA subject. --- ## The 2-Hour Weekday Study Block Consistency matters more than intensity. A structured two-hour study block is usually more effective than irregular long sessions. **Suggested weekday structure** 0:00–0:10 — Review yesterday’s weakest point (flashcards or notes) 0:10–1:00 — Application practice (write part of a past exam answer) 1:00–1:40 — Review one core framework or topic 1:40–2:00 — Plan tomorrow’s study session **Key rules** - Put your phone on airplane mode. - Study in the same location each day if possible. - If you miss a day, resume the schedule the next day rather than doubling your workload. --- ## The Weekend Study Block Weekend sessions allow deeper work and exam simulation. ### Saturday – Practice and Analysis Hour 1 — Review key frameworks from the week Hours 2–3 — Write a timed practice answer (45 minutes writing + 15 minutes review) Hour 4 — Gap analysis and targeted revision ### Sunday – Preparation and Consolidation Hour 1 — Review weaknesses from Saturday’s answer Hour 2 — Preview next week’s topic Hour 3 — Organise notes and materials Hour 4 — Light review or rest --- ## Balancing Work and NCA Preparation Many candidates preparing for the NCA exams schedule their study sessions the same way they schedule meetings — blocking time in their calendar. Some candidates describe their preparation to employers simply as **professional development**, which accurately reflects the purpose of the NCA process. Consistency and predictable study time are usually more important than the total number of hours studied. --- ## Managing Study Time With Family Responsibilities Candidates with family responsibilities often adopt one of two study schedules: **Evening schedule** Study after children’s bedtime (for example 9:00–11:00 PM). **Morning schedule** Wake early and study before work (for example 5:00–7:00 AM). Short daily sessions tend to be more sustainable than concentrating all preparation on weekends. --- ## Avoiding Burnout During NCA Preparation Balancing work and exam preparation can be demanding. Signs that you may need to adjust your study schedule include: - Persistent fatigue - Irritability or loss of focus - Difficulty maintaining consistent study sessions If this occurs, temporarily reduce weekday sessions to **1.5 hours** for a week before returning to the normal schedule. --- ## Common Mistakes Working Professionals Make ### Studying Only on Weekends Weekend-only study rarely provides enough repetition to master key legal frameworks. ### Passive Studying Reading textbooks without writing practice answers is usually inefficient preparation. ### Disorganised Materials Because NCA exams are **open-book**, candidates must be able to locate frameworks and legal tests quickly. --- ## Tools That Help With NCA Study Many candidates preparing while working full-time use simple tools to maintain consistency: - Flashcards for key frameworks - Timers for focused study sessions - Past exam questions for application practice - A study log tracking hours and topics covered --- ## FAQ: NCA Study Schedule **How many hours per day should I study for NCA exams?** Most candidates studying while working full-time prepare **2–3 hours per weekday** and longer sessions on weekends. **Can I pass the NCA exams while working full-time?** Yes. Many internationally trained lawyers complete the NCA process while maintaining full-time employment. **How many weeks should I prepare for each NCA subject?** Most candidates studying part-time prepare for **10–12 weeks per subject**. --- ## Final Advice The key to preparing for NCA exams while working is **protected study time**. Consistent daily preparation, organised notes, and regular practice answers are usually more effective than irregular long study sessions. With a realistic schedule and disciplined preparation, working professionals can successfully complete the NCA process. --- ## About the Author Kartik Kumar is an internationally trained lawyer who completed the NCA process and passed multiple NCA exams while working full-time.
