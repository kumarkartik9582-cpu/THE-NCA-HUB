Professional Responsibility is often the subject candidates leave until last, assuming their practice experience has prepared them. This is a mistake. The NCA exam tests the CBA Model Code of Professional Conduct specifically, and the ethical frameworks differ from those in other jurisdictions.

This guide gives you the structure to approach any PR question with confidence.

## The CBA Model Code Framework

The NCA tests the Federation of Law Societies' Model Code of Professional Conduct. You must know:
- Chapter 2: Relationship with clients
- Chapter 3: Relationship with the administration of justice  
- Chapter 4: Relationship with the profession
- Chapter 5: Relationship with the public

## Conflicts of Interest — The Most Tested Topic

Conflicts appear on almost every exam. The analysis follows a consistent pattern.

### Step 1: Identify the Conflict Type

**Concurrent conflicts:** Representing opposing parties in the same matter, or clients whose interests differ materially.

**Successive conflicts:** Current matter adverse to former client; using confidential information from previous representation.

**Imputed conflicts:** Conflicts of one lawyer attributed to the firm (with limited exceptions).

### Step 2: Check for Consent

Can the conflict be waived?

**Concurrent conflicts:** Requires informed consent of all affected clients, confirmed in writing.

**Successive conflicts:** Requires informed consent of former client (higher threshold), plus no use of confidential information.

**Important:** Some conflicts are non-consentable (e.g., representing opposing parties in litigation).

### Step 3: Withdrawal Requirements

If a conflict arises mid-representation and cannot be resolved:
- Must withdraw if continued representation would be unethical
- Must protect client confidentiality upon withdrawal
- May need court leave to withdraw from litigation

## The Professional Responsibility Answer Template

**1. ISSUE IDENTIFICATION**
- Identify the ethical dilemma (conflict, competence issue, withdrawal scenario)
- Note the specific Model Code rule implicated

**2. APPLICABLE RULE ANALYSIS**
- State the relevant rule from the Model Code
- Explain the purpose of the rule (protecting client interests, maintaining public confidence)

**3. APPLICATION TO FACTS**
- Apply the rule elements to the specific scenario
- Consider consent requirements (if applicable)
- Address imputation issues (if firm involved)

**4. SECONDARY ISSUES**
- Competence (if quality of representation questioned)
- Confidentiality (if information disclosure relevant)
- Withdrawal procedures (if leaving the file)

**5. CONCLUSION AND ADVICE**
- Clear statement of ethical obligations
- Practical next steps for the lawyer
- Risk mitigation strategies

## Competence and Confidentiality

### Competence (Rule 3.1)

Competence requires:
- Knowledge of relevant law/procedure
- Skill in applying that knowledge
- Thoroughness in preparation
- Diligence in handling the matter

**Common exam scenario:** Lawyer takes on matter outside their expertise without consulting senior counsel or referring out.

### Confidentiality (Rule 3.3)

Confidentiality is absolute (with limited exceptions like imminent harm).

**Key distinction:** Confidentiality (all client information) vs. solicitor-client privilege (communications for legal advice).

**Exceptions:** Required by law, authorised by client, or to prevent imminent harm (varies by jurisdiction).

## Withdrawal — When and How

### Mandatory Withdrawal

Must withdraw if:
- Client persists in illegal/fraudulent course
- Client uses lawyer's services to commit a crime
- Lawyer is incompetent to handle the matter

### Permissive Withdrawal

May withdraw if:
- Good cause exists (non-payment, breakdown of relationship)
- No material prejudice to client
- Reasonable notice given

### Procedure for Withdrawal

1. Give reasonable notice to client
2. Refund unearned fees
3. Return client property/documents
4. Protect client interests (deadlines, limitation periods)
5. Obtain court leave if litigation matter
