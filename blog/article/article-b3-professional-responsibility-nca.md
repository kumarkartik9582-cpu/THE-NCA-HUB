## The Lawyer-Client Relationship — Core Duties

The lawyer-client relationship is governed by several fundamental duties under the CBA Model Code.

### Duty of Competence (Rule 3.1)

A lawyer must provide competent representation, which requires:
- Knowledge of the law and legal procedures
- Skill in applying that knowledge
- Thorough preparation
- Diligence in handling matters

**Application:** A lawyer who takes on a matter beyond their expertise without seeking assistance may breach this duty.

### Duty of Communication (Rule 3.2)

A lawyer must:
- Inform the client of the status of matters
- Explain matters to the extent reasonably necessary for the client to make informed decisions
- Comply with reasonable requests for information

**Application:** Failure to return client calls or explain important developments may breach this duty.

### Duty of Diligence (Rule 3.2)

A lawyer must:
- Act with reasonable promptness
- Not neglect legal matters
- Keep clients informed of developments

**Application:** Missing deadlines, failing to file documents, or unreasonable delays may breach this duty.

## Confidentiality and Privilege

Confidentiality and solicitor-client privilege are related but distinct concepts.

### Confidentiality (Rule 3.3)

**Scope:** All information concerning the business and affairs of a client acquired in the course of the professional relationship.

**Duration:** Forever — survives the end of the lawyer-client relationship and the client's death.

**Exceptions (Rule 3.3-3):**
1. Express or implied client consent
2. Required by law or court order
3. To establish a defence in a controversy with the client
4. To collect a fee
5. To prevent imminent death or serious bodily harm

### Solicitor-Client Privilege

**Scope:** Communications between lawyer and client made for the purpose of seeking or giving legal advice.

**Purpose:** To encourage full and frank disclosure by clients.

**Exceptions:**
- Crime-fraud exception (communications in furtherance of a crime)
- Waiver by client

### Work Product

**Scope:** Materials prepared by the lawyer in anticipation of litigation.

**Protection:** From disclosure to opposing parties.

## Conflicts of Interest — The Bright Line Rule

Conflicts of interest are heavily tested on the NCA exam. The analysis follows a clear structure.

### Rule 3.4 — Conflicts

A lawyer must not act or continue to act where there is a conflict of interest.

**Conflict defined:** A substantial risk that the lawyer's loyalty to or representation of a client would be materially and adversely affected by the lawyer's duties to another client, a former client, or a third person, or by the lawyer's own interests.

### Concurrent Client Conflicts (Rule 3.4-1)

**Bright Line Rule:** A lawyer must not act for two or more clients in the same matter if there is a conflict.

**Exceptions (with consent):**
- The clients are represented by different lawyers in the firm
- The clients consent after full disclosure
- The lawyer reasonably believes able to represent both without acting adversely

**Analysis steps:**
1. Is there a concurrent client conflict?
2. Is consent required?
3. Was full disclosure provided?
4. Did both clients consent?
5. Can the lawyer represent both without acting adversely?

### Former Client Conflicts (Rule 3.4-2)

A lawyer who has acted for a client must not act against that client in:
- The same matter
- A related matter where confidential information could be used

**Analysis steps:**
1. Was there a previous lawyer-client relationship?
2. Is the new matter the same or related?
3. Could confidential information be relevant?
4. Did the former client consent?

### Personal Interest Conflicts (Rule 3.4-3)

A lawyer must not act where their own interests conflict with the client's interests.

**Examples:**
- Lawyer has a financial interest in the outcome
- Lawyer is a witness in the matter
- Lawyer has a personal relationship with an opposing party

## Withdrawal from Representation

Withdrawal is governed by Rule 3.7 of the CBA Model Code.

### Mandatory Withdrawal (Rule 3.7-1)

A lawyer must withdraw if:
1. The client discharged the lawyer
2. The lawyer is not competent to handle the matter
3. Continuing would violate the rules of professional conduct
4. The lawyer is unable to act in the client's best interests

### Permissive Withdrawal (Rule 3.7-2)

A lawyer may withdraw if:
1. Good cause exists (e.g., client refuses to accept advice, fails to pay, behaves dishonestly)
2. Withdrawal will not cause serious prejudice to the client
3. The matter is not before a tribunal (or tribunal consent is obtained)

### Notice Requirements (Rule 3.7-3)

When withdrawing, a lawyer must:
1. Give reasonable notice to the client
2. Refund any unearned fees
3. Return client property and documents
4. Provide information reasonably necessary for the client to find new counsel

## Answer Template for Professional Responsibility Questions

```
1. ISSUE IDENTIFICATION
   - Identify the professional conduct issue(s) raised
   - Note relevant CBA Model Code rules

2. DUTY ANALYSIS (if applicable)
   - Identify the duty engaged (competence, communication, etc.)
   - State the rule requirement
   - Apply to facts

3. CONFIDENTIALITY/PRIVILEGE (if applicable)
   - Identify confidential information or privileged communication
   - Check for exceptions
   - Determine if disclosure permitted

4. CONFLICTS ANALYSIS (if applicable)
   - Type of conflict (concurrent, former client, personal)
   - Apply bright line rule
   - Check for consent exceptions
   - Determine if conflict can be managed

5. WITHDRAWAL (if applicable)
   - Type of withdrawal (mandatory or permissive)
   - Requirements for withdrawal
   - Notice obligations

6. CONCLUSION
   - Summary of professional obligations
   - Recommended course of action
```

## Practice Question with Model Answer

**Question:**

> Lawyer L has represented Client A in a commercial dispute for two years. Client B approaches L to represent them in a similar commercial matter against a different party. L discovers that Client B's matter involves issues similar to those in Client A's case, and information learned from Client A could be relevant to Client B's matter.
>
> Advise L on whether they can represent Client B and what steps they must take.

**Model Answer:**

**1. Issue Identification**

This case involves a potential conflict of interest — specifically, a former client conflict. The issue is whether L can represent Client B given the previous representation of Client A and the similarity of matters.

**2. Former Client Conflict Analysis**

Under Rule 3.4-2, a lawyer who has acted for a client must not act against that client in the same matter or a related matter where confidential information could be used.

*Step 1: Previous lawyer-client relationship*
L represented Client A for two years in a commercial dispute. A clear lawyer-client relationship existed.

*Step 2: Same or related matter*
Client B's matter is "similar" to Client A's case and involves "similar issues." This suggests the matters are related.

*Step 3: Confidential information relevance*
The facts state that "information learned from Client A could be relevant to Client B's matter." This satisfies the test for potential use of confidential information.

*Step 4: Consent*
The facts do not indicate that Client A has consented to L representing Client B.

**3. Conclusion on Conflict**

L has a former client conflict under Rule 3.4-2. The matters are related, and confidential information from the Client A representation could be relevant. L cannot represent Client B unless:
- Client A provides informed consent, or
- L can establish that no confidential information would be relevant (which appears unlikely on these facts)

**4. Recommended Course of Action**

L should:
1. Decline to represent Client B, or
2. Seek Client A's informed consent (with full disclosure of the conflict), or
3. If L believes no confidential information is relevant, document this conclusion carefully

Given the similarity of matters and the explicit statement that Client A's information could be relevant, the safest course is to decline representation of Client B.
