## Stage 1: NCA Assessment

**What it is:** The National Committee on Accreditation evaluates your foreign legal education and experience.

**Outcome:** An assessment letter specifying how many NCA subjects you must complete.

**Typical requirements:**
- 5–9 subjects for common law jurisdictions (UK, Australia, US)
- 9–14 subjects for civil law jurisdictions
- Varies based on specific courses completed

**Timeline:** 4–8 weeks from application to assessment letter

**Cost:** $500 CAD assessment fee

---

## Stage 2: NCA Exams

**What they are:** Challenge exams in Canadian law subjects. Open-book, online-proctored, 3 hours each.

**Core subjects:**
- Administrative Law
- Constitutional Law
- Criminal Law
- Foundations of Canadian Law
- Professional Responsibility

**Elective subjects (varies by assessment):**
- Business Organisations
- Family Law
- Property Law
- Torts
- Contracts
- Civil Procedure
- Remedies

**Passing mark:** 50%

**Results timeline:** 10–12 weeks (typically 6–8 weeks)

**Exam fee:** $500 CAD per subject

**Sequencing strategy:**
1. Administrative Law first (most predictable)
2. Constitutional Law or Foundations second
3. Criminal Law third
4. Professional Responsibility last

---

## Stage 3: CPLED Legal Research and Writing (LRW)

**What it is:** A mandatory course in legal research, analysis, and writing. Not an NCA exam.

**Provider:** CPLED (Canadian Centre for Professional Legal Education) or provincial equivalent

**Format:** Online modules with assignments

**Timeline:** Self-paced, typically 3–6 months

**Cost:** Varies by province, approximately $1,500–2,500 CAD

**Note:** I completed the LRW course early — with approximately one month remaining before the deadline. This signals system efficiency, not just exam preparation.

---

## Stage 4: Articling or LPP

After completing NCA requirements and LRW, you must complete either:

### Articling

**What it is:** 10-month placement with a practising lawyer or law firm

**Requirements:**
- Find an articling principal
- Complete specified experiences
- Submit regular reports

**Timeline:** 10 months

**Compensation:** Varies widely, some positions are unpaid

### Law Practice Program (LPP) — Ontario only

**What it is:** Alternative to articling. Training program plus work placement.

**Components:**
- 4 months of training
- 4-month work placement

**Timeline:** 8 months

**Cost:** Approximately $7,000 CAD

---

## Stage 5: Provincial Bar Exams

After articling/LPP, you must pass your provincial bar exams.

### Ontario

- **Barrister exam:** Civil litigation, criminal procedure, family law
- **Solicitor exam:** Real estate, business law, wills and estates

**Format:** Open-book, multiple-choice

**Timeline:** 7 hours each (can be taken on separate days)

### Alberta

- **Practice of Law exam:** Ethics, practice management
- **Bar admission course:** Online modules

### British Columbia

- **Bar exam:** Multiple-choice and short answer
- **Professional Legal Training Course (PLTC)**

**Timeline and format vary by province.**

## Provincial Differences

### Ontario

- Largest legal market
- Most competitive for articling
- LPP available as alternative
- Two bar exams (Barrister and Solicitor)

### Alberta

- Smaller legal market
- Less competitive for articling
- Practice of Law exam
- Strong energy law sector

### British Columbia

- Growing legal market
- PLTC required
- Strong tech and international law sectors

### Other Provinces

Each province has its own law society with specific requirements. Check with the relevant law society for details.
