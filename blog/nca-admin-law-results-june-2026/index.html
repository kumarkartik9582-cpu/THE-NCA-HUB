NCA Administrative Law Exam Results Released — August 2026

By Kartik Kumar · 5 min read · Published: August 15, 2026

The NCA has released Administrative Law results for the June 2026 session. If you have been refreshing your NCA portal every morning for the past several weeks, the wait is over. Log in to your NCA portal to see your result.

Two outcomes. Two very different next steps. Both of them move you forward.

If You Passed

Congratulations. Administrative Law is one of the most conceptually dense NCA subjects — the Vavilov framework, Baker procedural fairness factors, the Doré distinction — and you navigated all of it under exam conditions. One fewer subject standing between you and your Certificate of Qualification.

Your immediate next steps:

  • Check your NCA portal — confirm the result is reflected in your assessment letter and that Administrative Law is marked as completed.
  • Register for your next subject — if you have remaining exams, check the 2026 exam schedule and register before the deadline closes. Do not wait. Momentum is everything.
  • If this was your last exam — you now need to complete the Legal Research and Writing (LRW) module and the Indigenous Intercultural Awareness course before the NCA issues your Certificate of Qualification (COQ). Check your portal for specific instructions and deadlines.

Do not let momentum fade. The candidates who pass all their subjects fastest are the ones who register for the next session immediately after getting a result. Open the Exam Planner and map out your remaining timeline now.

If You Did Not Pass

This is not the end. It is a data point. The NCA allows unlimited resits, and many candidates who ultimately qualify did not pass every subject on the first attempt. Administrative Law is widely regarded as one of the more challenging NCA subjects — the Vavilov framework alone has multiple layers that examiners test from different angles.

What matters now is what you do differently.

The Resit Timeline

The next Administrative Law exam session is August 31 – September 3, 2026. Registration typically closes around August 10. That gives you approximately 47 days from the date of this article to prepare.

That is enough time — if you use it strategically. The founder of this site passed Administrative Law with one week of preparation. Forty-seven days is more than sufficient if you focus on the right things.

What to Change for Your Resit

If you use the same approach and expect a different result, you will get the same outcome. Here is what needs to change:

  1. Diagnose where you lost marks. The NCA does not release marked papers, but you know which areas felt uncertain during the exam. Were you unsure about when correctness review applies under Vavilov? Did you confuse the Doré framework with standard Charter analysis? Did you run out of time? If you ran out of time, the problem is almost certainly answer structure — not knowledge.
  2. Use structured answer templates. NCA Administrative Law exams are open-book, 3-hour, essay-format exams. The candidates who pass are not the ones who know the most law — they are the ones who present their analysis in the format the examiners expect. Every answer should follow a consistent framework: identify the legal issue (standard of review vs procedural fairness vs judicial review availability), state the relevant rule (Vavilov para X, Baker factor Y), apply it to the facts given, and reach a conclusion.
  3. Practice under timed conditions. If you did not do at least two full timed practice exams before your first attempt, start there. Set a 3-hour timer, use only your permitted materials, and write out full answers. The exam tests retrieval speed as much as knowledge.
  4. Cut your materials down. If your notes are over 100 pages, they are too long for a 3-hour open-book exam. You need concise, tab-indexed reference notes that let you find the Vavilov correctness exceptions in under 30 seconds — not a textbook rewrite. The exam rewards fast retrieval, not comprehensive coverage.

The Admin Law Exam — What Examiners Actually Test

Based on community-reported patterns across multiple sessions, the most frequently tested areas in NCA Administrative Law are:

  • Standard of review under Vavilov — present in the vast majority of exams. You must know the five correctness exceptions and be able to apply them to novel fact patterns.
  • Procedural fairness under Baker — the five Baker factors appear regularly. Know each factor, and more importantly, know how to apply them when the fact pattern blends multiple factors.
  • The Doré distinction — when Charter values are at play in an administrative decision, examiners test whether you apply Doré (reasonableness) or whether you default to correctness review. This is a common trap.
  • Judicial review availability — privative clauses, statutory appeal rights, standing, and the distinction between jurisdictional and non-jurisdictional questions.
  • Remedies — certiorari, mandamus, prohibition, and declaratory relief. Know when each applies.
