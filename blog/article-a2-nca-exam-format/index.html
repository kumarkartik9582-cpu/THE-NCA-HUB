Exam Strategy

NCA Exam Format Explained: What Happens on Exam Day (2026)

By Kartik Kumar ·12 min read · Updated: 2025-11-01

Open-book, online-proctored, 3 hours. Exactly what happens on NCA exam day — room setup, question types, time strategy, and what to do if something goes wrong.

"Every exam is different."

"No idea what to expect."

"The format keeps changing."

These were the three most common phrases in the WhatsApp groups I monitored while preparing for my own NCA exams. They appeared dozens of times across thousands of messages. Exam format uncertainty is real, and it is one of the biggest sources of anxiety for NCA candidates.

This article exists to eliminate that uncertainty. By the end, you will know exactly what happens on exam day — from technical setup to time allocation to what you should have prepared before you log in.

The NCA Exam Format — What You Actually Need to Know

The NCA exam is:

  • Duration: 3 hours
  • Format: Open-book, online-proctored
  • Question count: Typically 3–4 long-form questions
  • Passing mark: 50%
  • Results timeline: 10–12 weeks (typically 6–8 weeks in practice)

The open-book nature is the single most important fact to understand. It changes everything about how you should prepare.

Open-Book Does NOT Mean Easy

This is the trap that catches most candidates. They hear "open-book" and think: I do not need to memorise as much. I can look things up during the exam.

This is catastrophically wrong.

The NCA is open-book because it tests application, not recall. The examiners know you have your materials. They write questions that require you to apply legal principles to novel fact patterns — not recite them.

You cannot read and apply simultaneously under time pressure. If you are searching through 300 pages of notes for the Vavilov framework while the clock ticks, you have already lost.

What open-book actually means:

  • Your materials must be organised and tabbed
  • You must know exactly where each framework lives
  • Your notes must be usable at a glance
  • You should never need more than 10 seconds to find a key concept

This is why The NCA Hub notes are under 80 pages. Everything you need is accessible. Everything else is noise.

Question Types — What You Will Face

NCA questions fall into three broad categories. Identifying which type you are facing within the first 60 seconds is a critical skill.

Type 1: Full Essay/Analysis Questions

Structure: Broad, theoretical question requiring comprehensive treatment of a topic.

Example: "Discuss the development of the standard of review in Canadian administrative law, with reference to the shift from Dunsmuir to Vavilov."

Approach:

  • Identify the core theme immediately
  • Structure your answer chronologically or thematically
  • Reference key cases and their contributions
  • Conclude with the current state of the law

Time allocation: 45–50 minutes

Type 2: Scenario-Based Problem Questions

Structure: Fact pattern followed by specific legal questions.

Example: "Sarah, a permanent resident, applied for citizenship. Her application was denied without reasons. She seeks judicial review. Advise Sarah on: (a) whether she has standing; (b) what standard of review applies; (c) what remedies she might obtain."

Approach:

  • Read the facts once quickly
  • Identify the legal issues raised
  • Apply the appropriate framework to each issue
  • Reach reasoned conclusions

Time allocation: 40–45 minutes per question

Type 3: Structured Short-Answer Questions

Structure: Multiple discrete questions, each requiring focused responses.

Example: "In relation to procedural fairness: (a) When is the duty triggered? (b) What are the Baker factors? (c) What remedies exist for breach?"

Approach:

  • Answer each sub-question directly
  • Use headings to separate responses
  • Do not over-elaborate — these are testing specific knowledge

Time allocation: 30–35 minutes total

The Online Proctored Setup — Exactly What You Need

The NCA uses ProProctor for remote proctoring. Here is your complete setup checklist:

Device Requirements

  • Computer: Desktop or laptop (tablets and Chromebooks are not supported)
  • Operating system: Windows 10/11 or macOS 10.15+
  • Browser: Chrome or Firefox (latest versions)
  • Webcam: Built-in or external, 720p minimum
  • Microphone: Required (for proctor communication)
  • Internet: Stable connection, minimum 3–5 Mbps upload/download recommended. Test your speed at speedtest.net before exam day. If below 3 Mbps, use a wired (ethernet) connection rather than WiFi.

Room Requirements

  • Private space: No other people present
  • Clear desk: Remove all unauthorised materials
  • Lighting: Adequate for facial recognition
  • Background: Clean wall preferred (proctor may ask you to show the room)
  • Mirrors: May need to be covered if visible

The Phone as Proctor Camera

This is the part that causes the most anxiety. Here is how it works:

  1. You install the ProProctor app on your smartphone
  2. At exam start, you position the phone to show your face, hands, and screen
  3. The phone remains connected throughout the exam
  4. The proctor monitors via this camera feed

Critical phone settings:

  • Airplane mode ON — this prevents calls and notifications
  • WiFi ON — the app needs internet to connect to proctor
  • Do Not Disturb: ON — this suppresses all notifications and calls during the exam. The ProProctor app runs independently of DND status.
  • Battery: Charge to 100% before starting
  • Position: Use a phone stand or stack of books — do not hold it

What happens if a call comes through:

With airplane mode on and WiFi on, calls cannot reach you. This is the correct setup. The ProProctor app functions normally regardless of Do Not Disturb status.

Time Allocation — The Formula That Works

Three hours. Three to four questions. Here is the allocation that works:

For a 3-question exam:

Phase Time Activity
Reading 5 minutes Read all questions first. Identify types. Plan order.
Question 1 45 minutes Write complete answer. Include all required elements.
Question 2 45 minutes Same structure. Same rigour.
Question 3 45 minutes Maintain quality. Do not rush.
Review 20 minutes Check for completeness. Add missing elements.

For a 4-question exam:

Phase Time Activity
Reading 5 minutes Read all questions. Identify types. Plan order.
Question 1 40 minutes Write complete answer.
Question 2 40 minutes Same structure.
Question 3 40 minutes Maintain quality.
Question 4 40 minutes Final question — do not rush the conclusion.
Review 15 minutes Check for completeness. Add missing elements.

The 5-minute reading phase is non-negotiable. Do not start writing until you have:

  1. Read every question
  2. Identified the question type for each
  3. Decided which order to tackle them (easiest first builds momentum)
  4. Noted any time adjustments needed

Reserve time for review. The last 15–20 minutes is where you catch incomplete remedies, missed elements, and careless errors. Do not sacrifice this time.

The Open-Book Study Strategy

Your materials must be exam-ready before exam day. Here is how to prepare them:

What to Tab

  • Core legal frameworks (Vavilov, Baker, Oakes, etc.)
  • Key statutory sections (Charter, Criminal Code where relevant)
  • Remedies tables
  • Answer template summaries

What to Annotate

  • Margin notes linking cases to frameworks
  • "See also" cross-references
  • Your own shorthand for quick recall

What to Leave Clean

  • Full case summaries (you will not have time to read these)
  • Background material (know this cold or remove it)
  • Anything you have not referenced in practice

The 10-Second Test

Before the exam, test yourself: Can I find the Vavilov framework in under 10 seconds? If not, your organisation needs work.

Technical Contingency — What If Something Goes Wrong?

Technical failures happen. I know this firsthand — my Foundations of Canadian Law exam was disqualified because my phone died mid-exam, cutting the proctor feed.

Here is what to do:

Before the Exam

  • System test: Do the ProProctor system test at least 48 hours before
  • Backup plan: Have a second device ready (charged, app installed)
  • Contact info: Save the NCA technical support contact (see nca.legal/contact for current information)
  • Documentation: Know how to screenshot/record if issues arise

During the Exam — If Issues Arise

  1. Do not panic. The proctor will contact you if they lose connection.
  2. Do not touch unauthorised materials while troubleshooting.
  3. Follow proctor instructions exactly.
  4. Document everything: Time, issue, what you did, proctor response.

After a Technical Failure

Contact the NCA immediately. Explain what happened. Provide documentation. Request an accommodation. The NCA has a process for this — use it.

My Foundations exam: Phone died at 2 hours 15 minutes. Proctor feed lost. Exam disqualified. I contacted the NCA within the hour, explained the situation, and requested a re-sit. The process exists because this happens.

Do not let technical anxiety stop you. Prepare for it. Then focus on what you can control: your preparation.

After You Submit — What Happens Next

Immediate

  • You will see a submission confirmation
  • Your exam is uploaded to the NCA system
  • You will receive an email confirmation within 24 hours

Results Timeline

  • Official: 10–12 weeks
  • Typical: 6–8 weeks
  • Notification: Email when results are posted to your NCA portal

If You Pass

  • Results appear in your NCA portal
  • You can register for your next subjects immediately
  • The pass is recorded permanently

If You Do Not Pass

  • Results include feedback on weak areas (limited but useful)
  • You can re-sit in the next available session
  • There is no limit to attempts
  • Many of our students come to us after failing — our method is designed to break through repeated failures

The Exam Day Checklist

One week before:

  • System test completed
  • Room prepared and tested
  • Materials tabbed and organised
  • Mock exam under exam conditions completed
  • Internet speed tested and confirmed above 3 Mbps

Night before:

  • Materials ready and accessible
  • Devices charged (including phone to 100%)
  • Alarm set (allow 30 minutes buffer)
  • Light review only — no new content

Morning of:

  • Wake with buffer time
  • Light breakfast
  • Final system check
  • Log in 15 minutes early

During exam:

  • 5-minute reading phase
  • Stick to time allocations (45 min/question for 3Q; 40 min/question for 4Q)
  • Reserve review time
  • Stay calm if issues arise
