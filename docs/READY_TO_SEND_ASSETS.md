## 2. GUEST POST (ready to publish — one credit link)

Offer this to any legal/immigration/newcomer blog. It's genuinely useful and non-salesy; the single link at the end is the backlink.

> ### The 2026 NCA Exam Schedule (and How to Prepare for Each Subject)
>
> If you trained as a lawyer outside Canada, the National Committee on Accreditation (NCA) is the gate you pass through before you can qualify here. The single most common planning mistake I see is candidates preparing around the wrong dates — so let's start with the facts.
>
> **How NCA exams are scheduled in 2026.** Contrary to a myth that circulates online, the NCA does not run "four sessions a year." It holds exam sittings in **most months of the year (January through November)**, and **each subject is offered on set dates several times**. For example, in 2026 Administrative Law sits in March, June, August/September, and November; Constitutional Law in January, April (late March), July, and October. Registration for each sitting closes roughly four to eight weeks beforehand. Always confirm your subject's dates on the official calendar at nca.legal before you register.
>
> **The format rewards technique, not memory.** Each exam is a **three-hour, open-book, online-proctored** written exam. The pass mark is **50%**, and results arrive about 10–12 weeks after the session. Because it's open book, the trap is thinking you can look everything up on the day — you can't; three hours is tight. The candidates who pass have tight, tabbed notes and pre-built answer templates they can deploy in seconds.
>
> **What each mandatory subject really tests:**
> - **Administrative Law** — the *Vavilov* standard of review (a presumption of reasonableness, rebutted in five situations) and procedural fairness under *Baker*. Learn those two frameworks cold and you've covered most of the exam.
> - **Constitutional Law** — the division of powers (sections 91–92, and the POGG, trade-and-commerce, and criminal-law heads) plus Charter analysis and the *Oakes* test under section 1.
> - **Criminal Law** — actus reus, mens rea, and the core defences; know the current law on self-defence, duress, and extreme intoxication.
> - **Professional Responsibility** — the Federation's Model Code: conflicts, confidentiality, and withdrawal.
> - **Foundations of Canadian Law** — legal method: the modern principle of statutory interpretation, the bijural system (common law plus Quebec's Civil Code), and *stare decisis*.
>
> **A realistic prep plan.** Budget about 100 focused hours per subject. Spend the first two-thirds learning the frameworks and building answer templates, and the last third doing **timed** practice questions under exam conditions — writing a full analysis in 45 minutes is a skill you don't want to attempt for the first time on exam day.
>
> **Two 2026 rules worth knowing.** Since 1 March 2026, an **Indigenous Law and Peoples competency** is mandatory before your Certificate of Qualification is issued (done via a CPLED module or approved course), and a **language screening** now precedes assessment for candidates whose legal education wasn't primarily in English or French.
>
> The NCA is passable, and it's more predictable than the internet makes it sound. Get your dates right, learn the frameworks, and practise under time pressure.
>
> *Kartik Kumar is an India- and UK-qualified lawyer who passed all five NCA subjects (four in under three months) and runs [The NCA Hub](https://www.thencahub.com/), a free resource hub and study-notes provider for internationally trained lawyers.*
