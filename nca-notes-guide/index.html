Are NCA notes worth it? An honest 2026 buyer's guide.

If you are internationally trained and facing the NCA challenge exams, study notes can save you weeks, or waste your money. Here is how to tell the difference, how much to pay, and how to choose, before you spend a dollar.

Are NCA notes worth it?

For most candidates, yes, but not for the reason people assume. The NCA exams are 3-hour, open-book papers with a 50% pass mark. Under that pressure the deciding factor is not how much law you can recall; it is whether you can find the right framework in seconds and structure the answer the way the examiner expects. Good notes buy you that. Bad notes, or 300-page textbook substitutes, actively slow you down on exam day.

The honest exception: if you already have a common-law background in a subject and can build your own tight, tabbed summary with answer templates, you may not need to buy notes for it. For everything else, well-built notes are cheaper than the time they save, and far cheaper than failing.

How much should NCA notes cost?

Across the market, NCA notes run from roughly $45 to $180 per subject. Price alone tells you little. The number that actually matters is the cost of getting it wrong:

  • A single failed sitting costs about $500 to resit, plus a three-month wait for the next session.
  • So the real question is not "can I afford the notes?" but "can I afford a resit?"
  • At $100 per subject, The NCA Hub notes cost about a fifth of one resit fee. If they prevent a single failed attempt, they have paid for themselves several times over.

Be wary of both extremes: rock-bottom notes are often outdated or bloated, and premium pricing does not guarantee they are built for the open-book format.

How to choose: five things to check

  • Length. Under 80 pages per subject beats 300. You cannot search a textbook in a 3-hour exam.
  • Answer templates. The single biggest differentiator. A pre-built structure for each question type is what turns knowledge into marks.
  • Exam-specificity. Notes should be built for the NCA challenge exam and its answer format, not repurposed law-school material.
  • Currency. Confirm they reflect the current NCA syllabus and recent changes.
  • A way to test before you buy. A free sample chapter and a readiness check let you judge fit without spending blind.

Do you need notes for every subject?

No. Buy only the subjects your NCA assessment letter assigns you. Most candidates get the five mandatory subjects (Administrative, Constitutional, Criminal, Foundations of Canadian Law, and Professional Responsibility) plus a few electives such as Contract, Family, Evidence, Property, or Civil Procedure. Paying per subject beats a one-size bundle you will not fully use. If you are unsure what you will be assigned, work it out first with a subject predictor.
